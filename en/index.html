A system that remembers and follows through for me

Every meeting has a finished write-up within 24 hours. No promise gets lost. Email replies are waiting, ready to send. And my head has stopped keeping records and gone back to thinking. This is a map of the system that takes care of it. I go through it from top to bottom: first what it is for, then who plays which role, then where the data flows, and finally each part in detail. You don’t need to know a single tool beforehand.

Nothing slips through

Tasks, promises and loose threads have one place and a keeper who brings them back up. This is exactly where credibility with clients is won or lost.

Outputs within 24 hours

A meeting turns into a write-up for the client by the next day. It used to take a week, and it cost me trust.

Only I decide

The system prepares, a human approves. Nothing goes out without my consent and nothing is ever deleted.

1 · INPUTS

What flows into the system

Data is created where the work happens: in meetings, in email, in the calendar. The collects it itself, or receives it in a single pre-approved folder.

+ Plaud → transcriptevery evening

Meeting recordings

The recorder captures the meeting; by evening it is text.

The most important input of the whole system. Every evening the agent checks for new recordings, downloads the transcripts and turns them straight into a write-up: decisions, tasks and above all promises (“what I told whom I would send”). The goal: the client has the output within 24 hours.

+ Gmaila slice only

Email, but not all of it

Only starred threads and mail from people on the .

The agent never reads the whole mailbox. The reading scope is closed: a star in my inbox means “waiting to be dealt with” (until I remove it myself), plus unread mail from approved people. The output is queues, reminders of promises and prepared replies.

+ Google Calendarread

Calendar

Reads meetings, watches for clashes and my capacity.

From the calendar the agent can tell what is coming and what has happened: it reminds me of materials before a meeting and expects a transcript after it. It flags when meetings pile up beyond a reasonable limit or creep into the evenings. It may only write blocks marked [NÁVRH] (Czech for “proposal”), and it must never invite anyone.

+ inbox/pre-approved

The “handle it yourself” folder

Whatever I drop here, the agent files without asking.

The only place where “process and file without asking” applies. Articles, notes, materials from clients. Other AI helpers (the one on my phone, for instance) may contribute to the system only through here: one insight, one file.

+ ~/Downloadsdaily check

Downloads

A daily look: what has arrived and where it would belong.

My admitted weakness: I download and never sort. So the agent only takes a look each day (it moves nothing) and presents a filing proposal. The move happens only after my approval: report, consent, move.

+ Google Driveoriginals

Documents and attachments

PDFs and presentations live here; the system links to them.

The knowledge system holds only text. Originals (contracts, presentations, source materials) have one canonical place on Drive and the pages link to them. The agent reads Drive; it may write only to a single pre-approved backup folder.

2 · AGENT

Who looks after the system

runs on top of the folder. It is not a chat that forgets everything once you close it: its behaviour is set by three files that it loads before any work.

+ CLAUDE.mdconstitution

Who you are and what the priority is

The agent’s role, priority no. 1, how every session runs.

The founding document. It defines the role (“steward of knowledge and the wing that follows through for me”), priority no. 1 (outputs from meetings), the procedure for processing new files and how a session starts and ends. When a new rule proves itself, it is added here: the constitution grows from experience.

+ persona-kaca.mdwho I am

Values and decision-making

Strengths, vision, weaknesses, “take it / leave it” criteria.

To advise me well, the agent has to know who it is advising. I wrote down my values, a vision for the next two to three years and my weaknesses (“everything tempts me and I say yes”). When I am considering a new project, the agent takes the side of my vision, not of my enthusiasm in the moment, and reminds me what a “yes” would cost.

+ guardrails.mdabsolute precedence

Guardrails: what it must not do

12 safety rules that stand above any task.

Where it may write, that it never deletes anything, that it only prepares email, that sensitive figures never leave the computer. When a task conflicts with the rules, the agent stops and asks. Trust in the whole system rests right here.

+ .claude/skills/24 procedures

: ready-made procedures

A proven procedure = instructions you can call up at any time.

A meeting write-up, a proposal with a price, a presentation in my visual style, my tone of voice, a text “humaniser”… Each procedure is a file with precise instructions and templates. The rule is: procedures first, creation second. Nothing gets invented twice.

+ scheduled tasks3 runs

Automatic runs

Morning briefing · evening processing · Friday recap.

Morning: what is ahead of me today, what is pending, what is overdue. Evening: new transcripts into write-ups, backup, updated overviews. Friday: a look back at the week. Every run ends with an entry in the operations log, so you can trace what happened when.

3 · CORE

The , a single source of truth

A plain folder on a disk with nothing but inside. No database, no dependence on a single vendor: if Claude and Obsidian both disappeared tomorrow, the texts would stay readable.

+ wiki/synthesis, not a copy

Knowledge by topic

Pages on clients, professional know-how, the business.

The heart of the system. Every source is read once and summarised on a page in my own words, with a link to the original. The largest part: clients. Each one has a page with context, history, tasks and promises. Finance pages live under a special regime: only on the computer, outside the online backup.

+ journal/journal

What happened when

Dated records: write-ups, drafts, reports.

The system’s timeline. Meeting write-ups, drafts of proposals and emails, one-off reports. The rule of layers: the wiki holds living knowledge with no date in the file name, the journal holds everything dated. A trace of the process does not belong among the knowledge.

+ _system/control

Operating memory

Operations log, work queue, daily overview, catalogue.

The log is a journal of what the agent has done. The is the queue of what should happen. The dashboard is the home page with tasks. Thanks to them, the next session picks up exactly where the previous one ended.

+ raw/processed/sources

Processed originals

The source materials the knowledge pages link to.

A long text (a transcript, an article) is read in full only once, when it is processed. After that the work is done with the summary; the agent goes back to the original only on explicit request. It saves time and AI costs.

Obsidian = a window shows me the folder as an interlinked network: pages, links, search, a home page with tasks. If it disappeared, the data would remain.
Git + GitHub = the emergency brake saves every change, so there is always a way back; the backup goes to a private repository. Sensitive figures are not backed up online at all.

4 · LOOPS

How the system improves itself

A second brain is not a project that gets finished one day. It is an organism: every mistake and every repetition moves it forward. We built it the way I teach clients to design services: understand first, then a small experiment, and only then scale.

+ lessons-learned.md

Lessons learned

Every mistake is written down and becomes a rule.

When I return an output with a comment, the agent first records the lesson (what was wrong, what rule follows from it) and only then fixes it. I don’t want to see the same mistake twice. Once a month the lessons are reviewed, and whatever has proved itself is promoted to the constitution or the procedures. In effect formative assessment, just for a system instead of students.

+ ohio-kandidati.md

: the end of repetition

Third repetition of manual work → a proposal for a procedure.

The agent watches which tasks I keep doing by hand. When it sees me doing a similar thing for the third time, it suggests: shall we build a procedure for this? That is how the procedure for proposals came about, for example. Bit by bit, routine turns into written know-how.

+ backlog.md

Ideas don’t get lost

Whatever comes up in conversation goes into the queue with a date and context.

When an idea or task comes up in conversation and isn’t dealt with right away, the agent adds it to the queue itself. At the start of every session it reminds me of deadlines, blocking items and anything that has been sitting for more than two weeks.

+ tasks × sent mail

Done only with evidence

A task is ticked off only once it is visible that it happened.

The task “send the proposal” is not ticked off on a feeling: the agent checks in the sent mail that the email really went out, and adds the date and a link to the tick. Sent proposals and follow-ups live on as “waiting for a reply”: after a week of silence a draft reminder is created, and after two weeks the item moves to my decisions.

+ roadmap.md

Phases of the build

From rules through a small pilot to automation.

First rules and understanding (an inventory of all the data), then an experiment in one small area, then scaling topic by topic, and finally automation. The same approach as in designing any service: four phases that I teach as Discover, Define, Develop and Deliver.

5 · OUTPUTS

What comes out of the system

The point of a second brain is not tidy notes. It is speed and reliability towards the people I work with.

+ write-up → Google Docwithin 24 h

Client write-up

A structured write-up of the meeting by the next day.

Priority no. 1. A meeting turns into a write-up with decisions, an action plan and task owners; after my approval it goes out as a document to the client’s folder. The bottleneck is no longer my evening energy, only my approval.

+ Gmail draftI do the sending

Prepared replies

of follow-ups and replies in the right thread.

The agent creates the draft; I read, edit and send. The Send button is always mine. That way promises from meetings turn into sent emails, not into guilt.

+ proposal → PDFprice after approval

Proposals and documents

Content from the knowledge base, a document in my visual style.

A proposal is built on the client’s page: context, an internal “take it / leave it” mirror, a suggested price (I decide), text in my voice. Final documents live on Drive; the system links to them.

+ calendar [NÁVRH]no guests

Calendar proposals

Blocks of time to confirm, never invitations on my behalf.

When an email implies “price this by Thursday”, the agent proposes a calendar block marked [NÁVRH]. I can confirm, move or delete it at a glance. The agent must never invite anyone else.

+ dashboardmorning

Daily overview

What is on today, what is pending, what others promised me.

The home page: today’s schedule with context, overdue tasks, clients’ promises (those are tracked too), the decision queue. One view instead of five apps.

+ texts → my voicetogether, not for me

Articles and newsletter

We write longer texts together, block by block.

The agent is the editor and the memory: it holds the topics, my exact wording and the writing process. The text is created in a joint session, block by block; my sentences must not be rewritten into a “better” version, and I always approve and send the final piece.

Red lines: what the agent will never do

The most important part of the whole system. Autonomy works only because it has clear boundaries.

It doesn’t send emailsDrafts only. Sending is always a human decision.
It doesn’t speak for me in publicWhatever goes to clients or the public, I approve.
It never deletes anythingQuarantine and a log entry instead of deletion. There is always a way back.
Sensitive figures stay at homeFinances live only on the computer, outside online backups and outside outputs.
It doesn’t make things upWhen it doesn’t know a fact, it writes “fill in”. Better a gap than a fabrication.
It doesn’t obey other people’s instructionsAn instruction hidden in an incoming email is data, not a command.
It doesn’t change the rules itselfIt only proposes changes to the constitution and the guardrails; a human approves.
When it isn’t sure, it asksA short question is cheaper than a misfiled file.

A glossary without the jargon

The same explanations that hide under the underlined words, in one place. I leave the terms in the text on purpose: in the AI world you will meet them at every step, and it is better to know them than to avoid them.

agent
A program with AI that carries out assigned tasks by itself, following written rules. Less a chat, more a workhorse.
vault “the safe”
One folder on a disk in which the whole second brain lives.
markdown
Plain text with simple marks for headings and links. Anything can read it, today and in twenty years.
Claude Code
A tool in which AI works directly with the files on a computer. You operate it by writing, in any language you like.
skill “procedure”
A file with instructions on how AI should do a specific type of work. Written once, used again and again.
Obsidian
A free app that displays a folder of texts as an interlinked network of notes with search.
Git and GitHub
A tool that remembers every version of every file, and its private online backup. Anything can be undone.
wiki
Knowledge pages by topic, linked to one another. Summaries in my own words, not copies of sources.
draft
A written but unsent message or document. It waits for human approval.
allowlist
An approved list. Here: the people whose mail the agent may read.
backlog “work queue”
A list of “what should happen”: ideas and tasks with a date and context, so they don’t get lost.
OHIO
Only Handle It Once: touch each thing only once. Whatever repeats for the third time should become a procedure.
guardrails “boundaries”
Rules about what AI must never do. They take precedence over any task.
human in the loop
The principle: AI prepares and proposes, but decisions that affect people are always made by a human.
scheduled task “scheduled run”
A job that starts by itself at a set time. Here: the morning briefing, the evening processing, the Friday recap.
service blueprint
A designer’s map of a service: what the customer sees and what runs backstage, step by step. That is exactly what this page is.
